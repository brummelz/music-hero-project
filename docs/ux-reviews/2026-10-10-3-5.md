# Child UX review: ages 3-5
Reviewed: Horn Hero v0.1 first working build. 12 screenshots of the real app driven by the keyboard simulator (01-cover-loading to 12-settings), plus `ui/game_screen.py`, `ui/home_screen.py`, `ui/theme.py` metrics and `core/feedback.py`. 2026-10-10. Spec and design mockups were not re-read for this pass beyond FR-16 / FR-18 / NFR-01 citations.
Evidence limits: static screenshots and code only, not live interaction. No real horn or microphone input (keyboard simulator). The success chime cannot be heard, so sound is unverified. Animation and timing are inferred from code only (gauge refresh about 15 ms with smoothing; hold ring fills over 3 s; 150 ms grace period); I did not see any motion. Screenshot "09-level-challenge" actually shows Level 2 gameplay (Re, 1.2 / 3.0 s), not a separate level-intro screen. Behaviour of the Back button mid-session, Reset progress confirmation and the audio-error text was not observed (unverified).

## Age 3-5
**Verdict:** The cover makes a 4-year-old smile and want to press the big button, but once the game starts the child is looking at a grown-up's tuner: a staff, a bar and a number, with no friend, no picture of what to do and a very small reward.

**What works**
- Cover (02): one huge "Start Game!" button (about 590 x 120 px), cheerful boy with horn, smiling microphone and animals. One obvious next action. Loading state (01) is calm and has a progress bar, not an error look.
- Home (03): "Do / Re / Mi" tiles are very large (about 210 x 205 px) and "Do" is pre-selected, with a giant "Start practice" button, so a child can press one big thing and get going.
- Silence (04): calm and inviting. Pale, desaturated gauge, dashed target ring, blue-grey bar, music-note icon, "Listening...". It does not look like an error.
- Direction is never colour alone (05, 06, 07): needle position, a highlighted pill with arrow and word, a coloured message bar with an arrow icon, and a check icon for in tune. Good for colour-blind children and for non-readers who only track position.
- Hold ring (07, 08): the ring around the note fills clockwise and turns solid green with a star icon at 3 s. A visible progress cue exists, and it is not a number.
- Messages are all positive and short (core/feedback.py). No "wrong", "fail" or "try again" in gameplay. FR-16 / FR-18 respected on the play screen.
- Level complete (10): large stars and a large "Next level" button.

**Problems**
1. [Major] Gameplay, whole screen - "Where is my friend? What do I do?" None of the cover characters (boy, mic, squirrel, rabbit) appear on Home, Play or the end screens. The play screen is a treble clef, five lines, a bar and text; the emotional anchor of the cover is lost and a 3-5 child has nothing to react to or to "play for". Likely behaviour: looks away, wanders, or only reacts to the grown-up. -> Fix: put a reaction character (the mic or the animals from the cover art) in a fixed corner of the play screen: sleepy/listening in silence, head-tilt in flat/sharp, bouncing/clapping when in tune and at +1 point. Reuse the cover assets (offline). Character state is driven by the same Feedback enum, so no new logic.
2. [Major] Gameplay (05-08), hold ring around the note - "The circle is tiny and it's on a music note I can't read." The ring is about 145 px in a 1650 px wide window (under 9% of width) and is the only visual for the 3-second hold; the same screen has a large numeric "1.9 / 3.0 s" which means nothing to a non-reader. -> Fix: make the hold progress a big element (e.g. a large ring or a row of 3 stars/balloons that fill one per second) at least 25% of the screen height, placed where the eye already is (above the feedback bar). Keep the number small or hide it for a "young mode" setting. Keep hold-time as the existing config constant (do not hard-code 3).
3. [Major] Gameplay (05, 06), arrow semantics clash - "The arrow pointing down says Sharp... and the arrow in the box points up?" On the same screen "Flat" carries a down arrow and "Sharp" an up arrow (gauge pills), while the message bar uses the opposite arrow for the instruction (flat shows an up arrow "push higher"; sharp shows a down arrow "come down"). A non-reader can only follow arrows, and here two arrows with opposite meaning sit in the same view. -> Fix: keep one meaning per arrow. Show only the instruction arrow (large, in the message icon, "go up" / "go down") and remove arrows from the Flat / Sharp pills, or use a different glyph (e.g. low/high bar ends) for state vs instruction. Cross-age issue.
4. [Major] Gameplay (06), "far" states use strong red - "Red means I did something bad." Sharp-far fills the label pill, the message bar and the gauge ends with red/pink; flat-far uses the same red. For a 3-5 child red is "stop / wrong", and there is nothing else to tell them it is a safe place to try again. FR-16 forbids any hint of loss or punishment, and the colour alone carries that hint even though the words are kind. -> Fix: replace red in the far zones and message bar with a warm non-alarming tone (e.g. soft orange / lavender) while keeping position + arrow; keep green as the single "yes" colour. Check contrast and colour-blind pairing remain intact.
5. [Major] Gameplay (05, 06, 07), hold progress after a dropout - "I was doing it and the circle emptied." The ring/counter show "0.0 / 3.0 s" the instant the note leaves the green zone (05, 06), so a wobble looks like total loss of progress (hold resets by default; the "pause instead of restarting" option is hidden in Settings under "Advanced"). Combined with a 150 ms grace period this is probably fine for a steady adult but a 4-year-old's breath will wobble constantly. Unverified in motion. -> Fix: do not change the rule (spec); change the presentation: when progress is lost, drain the ring/stars slowly (about 0.5 s) with a soft sound-free animation rather than snapping to zero, and keep a faint ghost of the previous progress. Also flag to the product owner whether "pause" should be the default for the 3-5 profile; this is a spec/default decision, not a silent change.
6. [Major] Gameplay, no reference tone - "What does Do sound like?" The child must produce a note known only from a printed label "DO / C" and a staff note. Neither the screenshots nor `ui/game_screen.py` show a "hear it" control. A pre-reader cannot use either. Likely behaviour: plays anything, hears only "keep trying". (Spec coverage of a reference tone: not checked, unverified.) -> Fix: add a big speaker button next to the target note that plays the target pitch once (offline synthesised tone, volume from settings) and show the note's solfege as a colour-coded big tile. Flag as a possible new requirement.
7. [Major] Gameplay (08), reward moment - "Did something happen?" The reward for 3 seconds of effort is a star icon in a 52 px circle, text "Great job! +1 point!" and one 10 px dot filling in a pill at the top right. The ring turning solid is the only large change. Celebration lasts 1.2 s in code and the chime is the only other cue (unverified). Words and "+1" are meaningless to a pre-reader. -> Fix: when a hold completes, fly a large star from the ring to the score and bounce the character (item 1); replace the 10 tiny dots with 10 big star slots (min 36 px) so progress is countable by a child. Keep the sound under the existing volume setting.
8. [Major] Home (03), mode choice - "Which one do I press?" The two cards are separated only by words ("Practice", "Adventure", sub-lines, "Hold the note steady for 3 seconds to score a point."), a 58 px icon circle each, and a padlock list. A pre-reader cannot tell what either does, and the Adventure card is a list of text rows ("First notes", "Five-note scale", "Full octave"). Without a grown-up the child can still press the large "Start practice", but cannot understand what they chose. -> Fix: give each card a big illustration from the cover art (e.g. mic + one note for Practice, path/flag with stars for Adventure), shorten sub-lines for adult readers only, and make the current level's "Play" the single dominant button. Level rows should lead with a picture or star count, not words.
9. [Major] Play and Home, settings reachable by a child - "A cog! Press it!" The 54 px gear sits on every Home and Play screen next to the score pill (and directly beside the Back arrow area on Play) and opens the dialog with no gate; the same dialog has "Reset progress...". A mashing 4-year-old can open Settings mid-hold, and the Escape key leaves the game instantly (game_screen.py). Reset progress confirmation: unverified. -> Fix: make Settings a press-and-hold (about 1.5 s) or small "grown-ups" gate (e.g. two-finger hold / arithmetic question), shrink the gear, and move it away from Back/score. Require a confirmation for Reset progress and for leaving a session with progress.
10. [Minor] Level complete (10), empty third star - "My third star is broken / missing." A dashed grey star plus "Try again to collect all 3 stars." reads as "you didn't get it". The level also still says "complete!", so no failure state, but the empty slot is the biggest object next to the filled ones. The star rule itself is spec (d6a7f15), so this is presentation only. -> Fix: show the missing star as a very faint filled outline (no dashes), animate earned stars in one after another, and move the "Try again..." sentence to a smaller adult-facing line. Escalate to Major if a 3-5 child is seen reacting to the empty star in live testing.
11. [Minor] Practice complete (11) and Level complete (10) - mostly empty white card; no character, confetti or picture. "You did it!" is the right phrase but a 4-year-old cannot read it. -> Fix: add the cover characters cheering, large stars/medal, and keep the two buttons (Home / Play again) but add icons (house, circular arrow).
12. [Minor] Practice length - 10 holds x 3 s is at least 30 s of continuous effort, plus 6 holds per level (FR-16). For a 3-5 child with seconds of attention this is long. -> Fix: do not change the counts silently; propose (to the product owner) a shorter "young" goal as a setting/config value. Within the current rules, make each completed hold feel like a finish (item 7).
13. [Minor] Cover (02) - "Start Game!" has no icon. -> Fix: add a play-triangle to the left of the label. (The Start button is otherwise excellent.)
14. [Minor] Play (04-08), clutter for adults - "Written pitch · Horn in F", "TARGET NOTE", "Hold for 3 seconds" (about 16-20 px, grey) are noise to a pre-reader and competing for attention with the real controls. -> Fix: dim or hide them in a young mode, keep them for grown-ups.
15. [Minor] Sound - the app gives no spoken prompts; the only sound is the success chime (not heard). Pre-readers get no audio explanation of "higher / lower". -> Fix: optional short recorded voice clips ("higher!", "lower!", "yes!") played offline, off by default, under the volume setting.
16. [Minor] Home (03), locked levels - dashed padlock rows look inactive/broken to a child. -> Fix: show locked levels as soft silhouettes with a small lock and no word "Locked".

**Would this child come back tomorrow?** Maybe. The cover and the green "yes!" cue are inviting, and a parent can set up a session in two taps. But after the cover there is no character, no spoken guidance and no large reward, so a 4-year-old is likely to be engaged mainly by the adult. With items 1, 2, 6 and 7 fixed the answer moves to yes.

## Cross-age summary
- Issues likely shared with other bands (only the 3-5 evidence here; other bands not reviewed): conflicting arrow meanings (item 3), reset-on-dropout presentation (item 5), settings gear accessible without a gate (item 9), red "far" zones (item 4). Items 1-3 and 7 are listed as Major for 3-5, and I expect 6-8 to want clear direction cues and a visible reward too.
- Conflicts: 3-5 needs larger icons, a character and a big ring; older children (12-15) may find a character babyish. Recommended resolution: make the character and big ring part of a "young player" age/profile option while keeping the compact tuner for older bands, rather than changing the single profile (spec: "single profile").
- Spec flags (do not change silently): FR-16 (no failure state): far-zone red and the empty third star (items 4, 10) are presentation risks, not rule breaks. FR-18 (messages) satisfied in text but only text, which a 3-5 child cannot read; consider icon/voice carriers. NFR-01 (100 ms): not testable from screenshots; with a ~15 ms tick the code looks compatible, unverified. Reference tone (item 6), shorter goals (item 12) and the hold-pause default (item 5) would be new requirements or default changes for the product owner to decide.
